Tethered 6U simulators

Three interactive simulators, in the order that best explains the mission: first the imaging concept, then the tether mechanism, and finally the technical analysis.

  1. 1

    How the two halves photograph the Earth

    A 3D scene in eight steps: launch, separation, why the pair hangs vertically, where each camera sits and where it points. It compares both cameras at nadir, adjacent strips, fore/aft stereo and gimballed cameras, with live camera views and the two photos used for 3D reconstruction.

    A 12° forward, B 12° aftB/H = 0.4331 s between photosheight error ≈ 3.5 m±30° gimbal: 635 km reachable
    Open the 3D simulatorinteractive/en/camera_concept.html
  2. 2

    Spring, deployment and retrieval

    The full mechanism cycle with the orbital physics of the pair: a spring separates the halves, winches brake and deploy 100 m, the system holds, the tethers are reeled in and magnets dock the halves. Includes the cases where retrieval fails.

    spring 0.2 m/sdeployment 99 minretrieval 5.9 hno control: tumbles at 36 m
    Open the mechanism simulatorinteractive/en/mechanism.html
  3. 3

    Tethered dumbbell simulator

    Five analysis scenes: deployment with free libration against the radial model, the cost of spinning in the inertial view, the thermal transient with and without compensation, tension sharing among three tethers, and what each camera sees.

    T_gg(100 m) = 0.719 mN19 slack events uncompensatedfirst tether slack at 0.0062°max torque 5.1×10⁻⁵ N·m
    Open the analysis simulatorinteractive/en/simulator.html

All three use the same model of the pair in a circular orbit at 550 km, checked by the tests in tests/test_reference.py. Scenes use a schematic scale where needed so things are visible; angles and figures are the design values. The 3D simulator needs an internet connection.
