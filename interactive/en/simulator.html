Tethered dumbbell simulator

Five interactive scenes built on the same model that tests/test_reference.py verifies: deployment with libration, the inertial view, the thermal transient at the terminator, the tension distribution across three tethers, and what the two cameras see. The equations are ported from src/dumbbell.py and the constants from src/constants.py.

The same initial conditions, integrated with two models. On the left, libration is frozen at φ = 0. This is the radial model of the reference table, L = L₀ cosh(√3 n t). On the right, libration is free, as in the equations in CLAUDE.md. The Coriolis term −2(L′/L)(φ′+n) tilts the tether backwards as it lengthens.

The dumbbell seen from an inertial frame. When it is aligned with the local vertical, the system rotates once per orbit, at rate n, and the gravity gradient holds it there at no cost. Any other spin rate needs angular momentum from the reaction wheels, plus a cyclic torque to keep the rate uniform. The tension each case requires is T = m_eq L(ω² − n²(1 − 3cos²φ)), with φ = (ω − n)t. If it comes out negative, the tether would have to push. The orange wedges are the cameras, pointed at nadir by the wheels of each half. With ω = n, the upper camera looks along the tether towards the lower half (scene 5).

At 100 m, with the system in equilibrium, the natural length of the tether changes by δ(t) = δ_max(1 − e^{−(t−20)/τ}) as it crosses the terminator. With a tether stiffness of EA/L = 31.4 N/m, the working elastic stretch is only 23 µm, so a few millimetres of thermal drift are enough to make the tether go slack. In the top strip the winch is fixed. In the bottom strip it regulates tension with ℓ′ = v_max · sat(G(T − T_ref)/T_ref). Displacements are exaggerated in the drawing and true to scale in the charts.

Three tethers between two 10 cm faces, 100 m apart. A relative rotation θ stretches some tethers and shortens others. The sum of the tensions is still set by the gravity gradient: two bodies joined only by tethers cannot pre-tension each other. This is why the torque the mechanism can apply is bounded by T_gg·d_max. The angle is exaggerated in the drawing.

The two halves sit one above the other because only alignment with the local vertical produces tension. That alignment is a design requirement. Each half points its camera at nadir with its own wheels, so both cameras see the Earth. The lower camera has a clear view. The upper camera looks down along the tether and sees the lower half 100 m away, in the centre of the image, with the tethers converging on it. The side view exaggerates the angles. The simulated image uses the true angles.

Model of two point masses in a circular orbit at 550 km, with a massless tether, m = 4 kg per half and m_eq = 2 kg. Fixed-step RK4 integration in the browser: 2 s for deployment, 2 ms for the thermal transient with a rigid tether, and 20 ms with the series element.
